# Three stories, three complete narrative treatments

User direction: 15 slides per story. Distinct themes need distinct narrative devices, pacing and graphic language. This replaces the idea of one generic decision template with three skins. Complete all three treatments and decks; no new approval gate is required.

For every slide, write: the audience's open question, the claim, concrete supporting content, the appropriate native composition, speaker notes and the transition. A component earns its place by explaining something. No fabricated measured results, prices or safety certifications. These are fictional proposals with explicit assumptions and decisions. Use meaningful detail rather than extra slogans to reach 15.

## Quidditch · an equipment selection hearing

Audience: captain, coaches and equipment keeper. Decision: authorize a fair trial and the evidence required before procurement. Dramatic tension: the glamorous fastest broom versus the equipment the whole squad can actually use. Narrative device: candidates audition for jobs. Voice: confident sporting expertise, specific rider tasks, never faux luxury advertising. Visual rhythm: atmospheric product image → role analysis → candidate portraits → comparable trial evidence → procurement decision.

| # | Audience question / claim | Concrete content and native composition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What are we selecting? A fleet for a team. | Photo cover; name the committee's decision. |
| 2 | Why not buy one celebrated model? Seven roles create different demands. | Bento/stat: three chasers, two beaters, keeper, seeker; role-specific tasks. |
| 3 | What would a good purchase change? Control, availability and rider fit. | Feature/points: observable outcomes, not unmeasured speed promises. |
| 4 | Who is in the running? Three candidates, three hypotheses. | Compact native comparison; Nimbus 2000, Firebolt, Cleansweep; status explicitly provisional. |
| 5 | What must Nimbus prove? Repeatable passing lines. | Split candidate portrait and chaser test; recovery after a turn, pass timing, rider comments. |
| 6 | What must Firebolt prove? Pursuit that ends under control. | Feature: pursuit, braking marker, recovery; no canonical performance numbers. |
| 7 | What must Cleansweep prove? Confident cover and training. | Feature-cards: substitute adaptation, position holding, equipment familiarity. |
| 8 | How is the comparison fair? Change the broom, keep the task. | Native graph: rider → shared course → observation record; equal nodes and legible labels. |
| 9 | What happens on the course? Turn, stop, then play. | Native flow: ordered trial stations, what the observer records at each. |
| 10 | What counts as evidence? Observations before rankings. | Native table with rubric: missed line / drift / recovery / confidence; unfilled result fields clearly marked pending. |
| 11 | What rejects a candidate? A failed control gate outweighs glamour. | Versus/checklist: purchase-worthy evidence versus sales impressions; stop criteria. |
| 12 | How might the fleet be allocated? Trial specialist and shared roles separately. | Native bar: actual squad role counts, proposed trial allocation; not procurement results. |
| 13 | Can we keep it flying? Maintenance is part of selection. | Photo bento: inspection, fault log, spare readiness, equipment keeper. |
| 14 | What do we need before buying? Evidence and supplier answers. | Checklist/table: trial notes, rider fit, repair support, lead time, quote; owners. |
| 15 | What is approved today? The trial, not an untested winner. | Split/takeaway: authorize course, shortlist, observers; return with comparison and quotations. |

Notes should connect each candidate's role to the same fair evaluation, so the middle does not become three advertisements. Distinguish the emotional photo moments from the evidence-bearing slides.

## ACME · the postmortem of a plan that catches its designer

Audience: ACME design review and its overconfident inventor. Decision: cancel machinery escalation, authorize an observation-first rehearsal. Dramatic tension: a beautifully specified mechanism rests on a bird doing something it never agreed to do. Narrative device: confident pitch → reveal → failure reconstruction → reversal. Voice: deadpan engineering precision with visual comic timing. Visual rhythm: theatrical opening → deceptively orderly mechanism → three-beat gag → sober diagnosis → smaller, credible plan.

| # | Audience question / claim | Concrete content and native composition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is promised? This time, for sure. | Photo cover with a short readable headline; the image supplies the absurdity. |
| 2 | What is the actual goal? Catch the bird, not merely fire the machine. | Versus: target outcome versus mechanism success. |
| 3 | What do we know? The bird can ignore the invitation. | Observations vs assumptions, explicitly distinguish fictional premise and things still to test. |
| 4 | What does the plan assume? Every arrow depends on cooperation. | Native graph, missing target-on-mark step prominent; browser-tested labels and clear connectors. |
| 5 | How is it meant to work? Bait, cue, release. | Flow of intended sequence; show trigger condition, not only boxes. |
| 6 | What actually happens first? Nothing takes the bait. | Native photo/feature, one strong beat; the empty mark is the evidence. |
| 7 | Who investigates? The designer enters the scene. | Annotated machine image: operator, trigger area, load; coherent spatial explanation. |
| 8 | Who gets caught? The wrong subject. | Revealed native flow, three-beat failure reconstruction; all reveal states readable. |
| 9 | Why did the mechanism fail the mission? It detects position, not identity. | Versus: component specification versus mission requirement; the actual causal flaw. |
| 10 | What makes a retry unacceptable? The operator is exposed. | Bento/annotate: load, controls, observation point; remove falling-load premise from rehearsal. |
| 11 | What are the alternatives? Escalation answers the wrong question. | Native table: catalogue order vs observation/rehearsal alternative and the uncertainty each resolves. |
| 12 | What should Revision B test? Whether the target follows the route. | Native graph/flow: marked route, inert prop, observer; deliberately simpler composition than slide4. |
| 13 | When does it stop? No bird, no second act. | KPI/checklist: inert prop, clear scene, stop condition; conditions, not results. |
| 14 | What must the next review receive? An observation log. | Native table: event, expected cue, observed subject, action; blank/example entries clearly illustrative. |
| 15 | What do we fund? Observation, not a larger anvil. | Takeaway/feature-cards: approve rehearsal, cancel escalation, name next evidence. |

Humor comes from cause and effect and the discrepancy between confidence and evidence. Avoid fifteen all-caps punchlines. Barlow Condensed remains readable; rejected stencil must not return. Preserve the graph labels' meaning without letting them hide beneath boxes in Firefox.

## Three Little Pigs · from three vulnerable houses to one shared refuge

Audience: the three siblings deciding what to protect together. Decision: agree a shared refuge brief and commission its design/survey before construction commitments. Dramatic tension: each sibling defends their own material and home, while the wolf exploits what none has considered together. Narrative device: three points of view converge into a collective plan. Voice: concrete, warm, story-led; architectural precision supports the stakes rather than replacing them with a generic consulting pitch. Visual rhythm: wolf-shadow story image → three individual perspectives → reveal of shared weak points → annotated model → staged collective response → acceptance and shared decision.

| # | Audience question / claim | Concrete content and native composition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is at stake? Three siblings need somewhere they can all reach. | Supplied house/wolf-shadow cover; introduce protection and collective choice. |
| 2 | Why are three separate plans insufficient? Everyone protects a different thing. | Native three-part composition: quick shelter, familiar timber home, solid wall; perspectives, no fabricated dialogue quotes. |
| 3 | What will the wolf exploit? An incomplete boundary. | Native annotation on the model: roof, openings, entrance; identify the drawing as a cutaway. |
| 4 | What do they agree to protect first? One usable shared refuge. | Versus: scattered renovations versus shared protected space; clear trade-off. |
| 5 | What does “usable” mean to all three? Reach, enter, close, remain together. | Native checklist/flow; accessible route and shared operation in the fictional brief. |
| 6 | What can straw contribute? Comfort inside the protected envelope. | Material detail crop, feature/points; proposal requiring design confirmation, not structural rating. |
| 7 | What can timber contribute? A frame with resolved connections. | Material detail and native annotation; roof/frame joins to be designed. |
| 8 | What can brick contribute? A shell with openings still to solve. | Material detail and contrast: wall alone vs continuous envelope; no claim of invulnerability. |
| 9 | Where does the boundary close? Roof and wall must work together. | Annotated model with two or three precise callouts and readable legend. |
| 10 | How do the pigs get inside? The entrance belongs in the defense plan. | Native flow: approach → entrance → closure → shared room; distinguish conceptual route from technical drawing. |
| 11 | What comes before finishes? Survey, design and usable enclosure. | Native tier-list priorities; avoid false dates or costs. |
| 12 | In what order can work proceed? Each stage has an acceptance condition. | Timeline: survey, detail, enclose, inspect; useful stage outputs, readable pending states. |
| 13 | Who looks after the unfinished items? Shared decisions need named roles. | Native table/bento: siblings as clients, designer, builder; responsibilities and unresolved questions, no fake contracts. |
| 14 | How do they accept the result? Walk it, operate it, record defects. | Checklist: entrance/closures/design conformity/open-item owner; criteria not claimed completed tests. |
| 15 | What do they agree today? One brief, one refuge, three people inside. | Image-led closing and explicit ask: approve brief and survey/design next step; return to opening stakes. |
